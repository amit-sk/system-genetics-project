Section 4: Combining QTL and eQTL results
=========================================

Definitions. The QTL scan used all 3,796 SNPs, whereas each eQTL scan used
the representative SNPs of its own strain set. The comparison is therefore
made over all 3,796 SNPs: a SNP is an eQTL SNP of a tissue if the
representative SNP that stood in for it (identical genotypes across that
tissue's strains) has at least one significant eQTL. A SNP is a QTL SNP if
it is significant (q < 0.05) for phenotype 881 or 1231 (24 + 38 = 62 SNPs,
on different chromosomes).

Question 1 - SNPs that are both QTL and eQTL
--------------------------------------------
Yes: all 62 QTL SNPs are eQTL SNPs in at least one tissue - 56 in both
tissues, 2 in the hypothalamus only, 4 in the liver only, 0 in neither.
Per phenotype: 881 (Chr 6 QTL) - 22 of 24 SNPs are eQTLs in each tissue
(20 in both); 1231 (Chr 5/10/11 QTLs) - 36 of 38 in the hypothalamus, all
38 in the liver.
Over the whole genome the three sets are: QTL only 0; eQTL hypothalamus
only 262; eQTL liver only 439; eQTL in both tissues 2,887; QTL and both
eQTL sets 56; QTL and hypothalamus eQTL 2; QTL and liver eQTL 4; none 146.
Thus 3,650 of 3,796 SNPs (96%) are eQTL SNPs in at least one tissue, and
the QTL SNPs (100%) are not markedly enriched above this background.

Question 2 - Added value of expression data for identifying QTLs
----------------------------------------------------------------
For *identifying* QTLs - deciding which SNPs are associated with the
phenotype - the expression data added little here: with 40-45 strains,
thousands of genes and eQTLs defined at the level of representative SNPs,
96% of all SNPs are an eQTL of some gene, so "being an eQTL" does not
discriminate QTL SNPs from the rest of the genome. The QTLs were found by
the phenotype scan alone, and every QTL SNP would have passed an eQTL
filter, but so would almost every non-QTL SNP. The 96% figure depends on
our definitions: because every significant SNP of a gene counts as an
eQTL, a strong cis eQTL marks its whole linkage neighbourhood (median 4
markers per gene), and the sparse marker map (one representative SNP per
~1.6 Mb, i.e. 15-20 genes) means almost every marker window contains some
gene with an eQTL. With one eQTL SNP per gene the coverage would be
roughly 40% of the representative SNPs per tissue and ~60% of the genome
for both tissues combined - lower, but the conclusion would be the same.

The added value is in *interpreting* the QTLs: a QTL is a region of
several Mb containing tens of genes and the phenotype scan cannot say
which of them matters. The eQTLs narrow this down to the genes whose
expression is genetically controlled from the QTL region in a relevant
tissue - e.g. Nfu1, Nup210 and Anxa4 in the Chr 6 striatal-volume QTL, or
Cyp3a25 (a cytochrome P450 drug-metabolising enzyme) in the liver at the
Chr 5 morphine-locomotion QTL - and they provide the intermediate
phenotype needed to test whether the QTL acts through gene expression
(Section 5). Expression data are therefore of little value as a filter for
QTL discovery in this setting, but of substantial value for moving from a
QTL to candidate genes and mechanisms.

Question 3 - Limiting GWAS to variants associated with an expression trait
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Positive sides:
- Prioritisation and interpretability: a variant that is an eQTL comes
  with a candidate gene and a mechanism (altered expression), which is
  what is needed for follow-up. GWAS hits are enriched for regulatory
  variants (lecture: Hindorff et al. 2009).
- Power: if the eQTL set is a small fraction of all variants, restricting
  the scan reduces the multiple-testing burden and lets weaker effects
  pass the significance threshold.
Negative sides:
- No reduction in practice: in our data 96% of SNPs are eQTLs for at
  least one gene when two tissues and thousands of transcripts are
  tested, so the filter removes almost nothing and the power gain is
  nil. With more tissues it would approach 100%.
- Loss of true associations: variants acting through mechanisms other
  than steady-state mRNA level (coding changes, splicing, protein
  stability, non-coding RNA) or through expression in a tissue, cell type,
  developmental stage or condition that was not profiled would be
  excluded. The eQTL set depends entirely on which tissues were measured.
- Linkage, not causality: in BXD mice an eQTL SNP tags a several-Mb
  haplotype block, and that the block contains an eQTL does not mean the
  expression change is on the causal path to the phenotype (this is what
  Section 5 tests).
Conclusion: expression associations should be used to annotate and rank
GWAS variants and to choose candidate genes, not as a hard pre-filter for
which variants are tested.

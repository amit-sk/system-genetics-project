Section 3: eQTL analysis
========================

Question 1 - Preprocessing
--------------------------
Both series were downloaded with GEOparse and the normalised values
deposited in GEO were used: hypothalamus GSE36674 (Affymetrix Mouse Gene
1.0 ST, GPL6246; 89 arrays; RMA log2 intensities) and liver GSE17522
(Agilent G4121A, GPL6466; 122 arrays; LOWESS-normalised log2 ratios
sample/reference). Steps c-f were applied to the array-level matrix
(79 arrays per tissue), before averaging per strain (step h).

a-b) Input matrix. For each series, the VALUE column of every sample (GSM)
   was joined into a probes x arrays matrix; the strain of each array was
   read from the 'strain:' field of the sample characteristics. The matrix
   was merged with the platform table (GPL) to obtain, per probe, the gene
   symbol, chromosome and position.
   - Hypothalamus: only 'main' transcript clusters were kept (28,101; the
     rest are normalisation/control probes). The platform has no plain
     gene-symbol column; the symbol was parsed from the 'gene_assignment'
     field ('RefSeq // Symbol // name // cytoband // Entrez /// ...'). When
     a cluster is assigned to several genes (~3,800 clusters) the first
     entry was used, as it is Affymetrix's primary assignment; discarding
     these clusters would lose genuine genes.
   - Liver: non-control probes were kept (20,868); GENE_SYMBOL and the
     probe location 'chrN:start-end' were used.
   Only arrays of BXD strains present in the genotype file were kept
   (79 arrays in each tissue). Dropped: the parental strains, F1 crosses,
   BXD92A (hypothalamus) and BXD11TY (liver), which are not in the
   genotype file.
   Genome build: the hypothalamus platform is annotated on GRCm38 (mm10),
   whereas the genotype file and the liver platform use NCBI Build 37
   (mm9). Hypothalamus gene positions were converted to mm9 with the UCSC
   liftOver chain (pyliftover); 124 genes did not map and have no position.
   Genes without a position (124 hypothalamus, 80 liver) are kept in the
   eQTL scan but cannot be classified as cis or trans.
c) Rows without a gene symbol were removed: 26,710 probes remained in the
   hypothalamus, 19,272 in the liver.
d) Low maximal value: the two platforms are on different scales
   (log2 intensities vs. log2 ratios), so a common absolute threshold is
   not meaningful. The threshold was set at the 10th percentile of the
   per-probe maximal values of each dataset (histograms in the notebook):
   max >= 6.10 (hypothalamus; the bottom tail of the RMA distribution,
   i.e. probes at background level in every array) and max >= -0.68
   (liver; probes at least ~1.6-fold below the reference in every array).
   Remaining: 24,042 and 17,348 probes.
e) Low variance: of the probes remaining after step d, those in the lowest
   10% of the variance distribution (variance across the 79 arrays;
   var < 0.0089 hypothalamus, < 0.021 liver) were removed. These probes
   are at the level of technical replicate noise of the arrays (SD ~0.1
   log2 units), so they cannot show an eQTL and only add to the
   multiple-testing burden; a 10% cut, symmetric to step d, removes this
   noise-floor tail without discarding genes with genuine but modest
   variation. Remaining: 21,637 and 15,613 probes.
f) Multiple probes per gene were collapsed by keeping the probe with the
   highest variance, which is the most informative for an association
   test; averaging probes would dilute a well-measured probe with poorly
   hybridising or saturated ones. Result: 19,577 genes (hypothalamus) and
   14,659 genes (liver).
g) The genotype file was restricted to the strains present in each
   expression dataset (45 hypothalamus, 40 liver). Consecutive SNPs with an
   identical genotype vector across these strains (unknown calls counted
   as a distinct value) give identical association statistics, so each run
   of identical neighbours was represented by one SNP: 1,598 representative
   SNPs for the hypothalamus strains and 1,403 for the liver strains (out
   of 3,796). The choice of SNP within a run does not affect the test and
   only fixes the position that represents the run; the first (most
   proximal) SNP was used, and the genomic span of each run was recorded
   (median 0.4-0.5 Mb, up to ~10 Mb) so that SNP-gene distances (cis/trans)
   can be measured to the run interval rather than to a single arbitrary
   SNP.
h) Arrays of the same strain were averaged regardless of sex (34 of the 45
   hypothalamus strains and 38 of the 40 liver strains have one array of
   each sex; the rest are single-sex), giving the final input matrices:
   19,577 genes x 45 strains (hypothalamus) and 14,659 genes x 40 strains
   (liver).


Question 2 - eQTL association test and choice of the final eQTL collection
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Test: the association test of Section 2 (eQTL mapping = QTL mapping with
the expression of a gene as the trait): linear regression of the strain
means of a gene on the genotype (B = 0, H = 1, D = 2) and F-test of the
regression, for every gene x representative SNP pair. Hypothalamus:
19,577 x 1,598 = 31.3 million tests; liver: 14,659 x 1,403 = 20.6 million.

Multiple testing: BH-FDR was applied once over all gene x SNP tests of a
tissue (q-value per pair), rather than per gene, so that a single
false-discovery level applies to the whole eQTL collection and the number
of significant eQTLs is well defined.

Selection of eQTLs: every (SNP, gene) pair with q < 0.05 is an eQTL of
that gene; genes with no significant SNP were excluded. We did not reduce
each gene to its single best SNP, since that would assume that all
significant SNPs of a gene mark the same locus, which need not hold (a
gene can have a cis eQTL and an independent trans eQTL). The eQTL position
is the representative SNP together with the span of its run of identical
SNPs.
Caveat: neighbouring SNPs that are highly (but not perfectly) correlated
across the strains are kept as separate eQTLs, so a single locus is
typically represented by several SNPs (median 4 significant SNPs per gene,
max 23), which inflates the number of eQTLs relative to the number of
independent loci.

Result (results/eqtl_<tissue>.csv):
  Hypothalamus: 7,509 eQTLs (SNP-gene pairs) for 1,416 genes (of 19,577
                tested) at 1,317 distinct SNPs (of 1,598).
  Liver:        6,804 eQTLs for 1,285 genes (of 14,659 tested) at 1,209
                distinct SNPs (of 1,403).


Question 3 - Results analysis
-----------------------------
Definitions: an eQTL (SNP-gene pair) is cis-acting if the gene is on the
same chromosome within 2 Mb of the SNP's run of identical markers, and
trans-acting otherwise; pairs whose gene has no genomic position are
'unknown'. Counts below are per tissue (figures in the notebook).

a) Number of eQTLs, cis vs trans
                                 Hypothalamus      Liver
   eQTLs (SNP-gene pairs)              7,509      6,804
     cis                        3,066 (41%)   2,320 (34%)
     trans                      4,434 (59%)   4,451 (65%)
     unknown                             9         33
   distinct eQTL SNPs                  1,317      1,209
     cis only / trans only / both  245/388/681  184/364/652
   genes with an eQTL                  1,416      1,285
     with a cis / a trans eQTL     1,128/1,112    872/1,087
   Most eQTL SNPs act both in cis (on a nearby gene) and in trans (on
   distant genes); ~80% of eQTL genes have a cis eQTL.

b) Genes per eQTL. Of the representative SNPs, 1,317 (hypothalamus) and
   1,209 (liver) are associated with at least one gene (281 and 194 with
   none). The distribution is heavily right-skewed (log-scale histogram
   in the notebook): 20% of the eQTL SNPs are associated with a single
   gene and about half with at most 3 genes (49% / 46%), the median is 4
   and the mean 5.7 / 5.6, while 14% of the eQTL SNPs are associated with
   more than 10 genes, up to 50 (hypothalamus) and 69 (liver). Most eQTLs
   thus affect one or a few genes, and a small tail of SNPs affects many -
   the trans hotspots examined in (e). The two tissues are strikingly
   similar in this respect (all fractions within 3 percentage points,
   same median and nearly the same mean), despite different platforms,
   strain sets and gene sets, suggesting that the shape of the
   distribution reflects the genetic architecture of expression in BXD
   mice rather than tissue-specific features.

c) Along the genome (notebook figure), the number of associated genes per
   SNP is not uniform: most SNPs have a few genes, and a handful of
   regions stand out - hypothalamus: distal Chr 17 (~22-37 Mb; up to 50
   genes), Chr 11 (~65 Mb), Chr 2 (~115-135 Mb), Chr 1 and Chr 7; liver:
   Chr 12 (~104-110 Mb; 69 genes), Chr 17, Chr 2, Chr 4 and Chr 8. These
   are the trans hotspots analysed in (e); adjacent SNPs in a region show
   similar counts because they are in linkage.

d) P-value distributions. Cis eQTLs are much stronger than trans eQTLs:
   median -log10(P) 7.2 vs 6.1 (hypothalamus) and 7.5 vs 6.0 (liver);
   the cis distribution has a long tail (up to 10^-29 / 10^-34) whereas
   trans P-values pile up just above the significance boundary
   (-log10 P ~ 4.9). This matches the lecture's observation that cis
   eQTLs tend to have larger effects than trans eQTLs; a single
   regulatory variant acts directly on the nearby gene, whereas trans
   effects are indirect and diluted.

e) eQTL map (SNP position vs gene position, notebook figure). Cis eQTLs
   form a sharp diagonal (gene at the SNP's position) in both tissues;
   trans eQTLs are scattered off the diagonal and concentrate in vertical
   bands - SNPs associated with many distant genes, i.e. trans-acting
   hotspots. Hotspots were defined as SNPs whose number of trans genes
   exceeds the Poisson expectation for a uniform spread of all trans eQTLs
   over the representative SNPs (P < 0.001): > 9 genes (hypothalamus;
   mean 2.8 trans genes per SNP) and > 10 genes (liver; mean 3.2).
   Hotspot SNPs within 5 Mb were merged into regions.
   Hypothalamus: 118 hotspot SNPs in 29 regions; strongest
     Chr 17: 22-37 Mb (peak 40 trans genes, rs13482930),
     Chr 11: 65-75 Mb (27), Chr 7: 71-74 Mb (24), Chr 17: 9-12 Mb (23),
     Chr 12: 27-29 Mb (22), Chr 2: 114-136 Mb (22).
   Liver: 93 hotspot SNPs in 30 regions; one dominant hotspot at
     Chr 12: 104-110 Mb (peak 64 trans genes, rs13481620), then
     Chr 17: 27-41 Mb (22), Chr 2: 107-126 Mb (21), Chr 8: 75-78 Mb (20),
     Chr 11: 72-75 Mb (19).
   Several hotspot regions are shared by the two tissues (Chr 17 ~27-37
   Mb, Chr 11 ~65-75 Mb, Chr 2, Chr 17 ~9-12 Mb, Chr 1 ~126-130 Mb),
   whereas the Chr 12 hotspot is liver-specific.
